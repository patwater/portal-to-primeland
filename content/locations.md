# Seventeen Locations in Primeland

*Worldbuilding notes for* A Portal to Primeland. *Seventeen numbered places, since 17 is prime, plus four unnumbered places from the first draft where every journey begins. Residents named here are described in `characters.md`, and the world's voice and values come from `SOUL.md`. Where these notes say "the traveler" and "the companion," they mean each family's own kid and pet (Taylor and Scout in the first-draft edition).*

---

## Where the Story Begins

### The Long and Lonesome Hill

This hill is on our side of the box, under the hot summer sky. An old delivery man in striped shorts and a matching smock drags a big blue nylon cube up it, with an arrow on its side pointing to A′. Brock tears the thingamajig out of its last box and races off, and the delivery man leaves the cube behind, empty. So it seemed. In a family's own edition the cube may turn up somewhere else, such as a front porch or a park, and that place takes the hill's role.

### The Bubble-Wrap Landing

When the bottom of the cube springs open, travelers fall onto a giant pile of bubble wrap tied up in ticker tape that reads "welcome to Primeland." The tape is a strip torn from the Adding Machine's endless roll. Every journey lands here.

### The Scissor Gate

Just past the landing, Chris and Steve the scissors cut every arriving number down until it cannot be divided any further, and only then do they let it in. Quo the Quokka tallies how many times each number fits, every even number gets a "2" stamped on its hand, and every arrival is asked "What's your count?" The Adding Machine stands just outside, cranking away, because its badge of 1 keeps it from ever quite getting in. Remainder Row begins right past the gate.

### The Data Lake

The Data Lake is a glittering, gleaming lake with a shimmering metal structure rising from its middle, fed by springs that seep up from the Data Cave far below. Patch the Webmaster strings fine metallic fiber across all of it so that information flows point to point. The Robo-Seals swim it inspecting everything, Sparky the Electric Eel keeps its lights on, Xavi the X-Ray Fish swims its depths, and Nega lives upside down beneath it. Terminus waits on the far shore.

## The Heart of Primeland

### 1. The Tower of Two

The Tower of Two stands in the very center of Primeland, and it is shaped exactly like a giant numeral 2. Its long flat foot rests on the ground with a round door at one end. A spiral staircase winds up the slanted neck and out along the curl to the pinnacle at the top, where a lantern blinks twice, pause, twice again, so that anyone lost in Primeland can find their way back to the middle.

The locals call Two "the oddest prime of all" because it is the only even one, and every even number that passes through the Scissor Gate gets a "2" stamped on its hand first. On the lobby floor, two hallways are tiled in the largest tile that fits both of them evenly, which the caretaker proudly calls the Greatest Common Tile. Halfway up the stairs is the Least Common Landing. If one climber hops two steps at a time and another hops three, the first place they both land is step six, and that is where everybody stops for lemonade.

### 2. Protocol Plaza

Protocol Plaza is the town square at the foot of the Tower, and it is where Chris and Steve the scissors go to fret about rules. A great chalkboard covers one whole wall of the plaza, and every rule in Primeland is written on it in chalk rather than carved in stone. Anyone can walk up and propose a change, but there is a rule (written at the very top) about how rules get changed, and the scissors guard that one most fiercely of all. Children draw hopscotch squares in the margins, and nobody seems to mind.

Alpha and Beta run their A or B tests here, holding up two of everything for passersby. The plaza gives the "proper protocols?" margin note a home, and it is a place where the traveler can learn that rules are things people make together.

### 3. Lamplight Road

Lamplight Road starts at the Tower's round door and runs straight out toward the horizon without ever ending. A lamppost stands at every prime-numbered step. Near the Tower the lamps crowd close together, but the farther you walk the longer the dark stretches between them become. Still, if you keep walking, another lamp always appears eventually.

Here and there along the road stand the Twin Cottages, pairs of houses only two steps apart (11 and 13, 17 and 19, 41 and 43), where neighbors wave to each other over the fence. Nobody in Primeland knows whether the twin cottages go on forever, and the old-timers argue about it every evening on their porches. That argument is the real unsolved twin prime conjecture, and the road echoes the first draft's unfinished opening line, "She lived next door to a..."

## Underground

### 4. The Mines of Many Primes

The Mines of Many Primes burrow into a hillside behind the Tower, and their entrance is a stone arch called Dig in the Dots, where glittering dots spray out of the dark like sparks from a grinding wheel.

Miners work the tunnels in pairs called Coprime Crews. The two partners in each crew share nothing in common at all (one hums while the other whistles, one likes mornings while the other likes midnight), and that is exactly why they never get in each other's way.

Every so often the foreman declares that the mine is finally tapped out. Then a miner multiplies every prime found so far, adds one, and a brand new tunnel cracks open, which is Euclid's old proof that the primes never run out. The miners carry the dots to the surface in buckets, because their motto is "collect the dots to connect the dots," and Patch strings each one into his web.

### 5. The Data Cave

Below the deepest tunnel of the mines lies the Data Cave, where nobody has bothered to sort anything. Numbers drip from the stalactites one at a time and collect in dark pools on the floor. Those pools seep upward through cracks in the rock and become the springs that feed Patch's Data Lake, so whatever trickles down here eventually shows up there. If you whisper a question into the cave, a hundred answers echo back, and only some of them are true. The companion will not go past the first bend, and that seems wise.

## The Waters

### 6. Seventh Falls

Seventh Falls pours from a cliff shaped like an enormous 7, and the water spills off the crossbar in a long sweeping curtain. The lake water always splits into exactly seven ribbons on the way down, and the mist throws a seven-colored rainbow every afternoon. A narrow ledge runs behind the curtain of water, and if you stand there you can count the ribbons one at a time, which makes it a good spot for a toddler to practice counting to seven.

### 7. Qubit Quay

Qubit Quay is a little harbor below the falls where the boats are both tied up at the dock and out at sea until somebody looks. The lighthouse is both on and off. The harbormaster gave up keeping a logbook long ago, and "maybe" is now the official answer to every question asked at the quay. Qubit spins at the end of the pier. This place could quietly foreshadow the flickering blip in Sector N2 that is neither stamped nor rejected.

### 8. The Platypus Flats

The Platypus Flats are a stretch of mud where the river slows and spreads out, and they are home to creatures that refuse to fit into any box. A platypus has a duck's bill and a beaver's tail, and it lays eggs and still feeds its babies milk. The robo-seals cannot stamp a platypus approved or rejected, so they freeze up and yelp whenever one waddles by. Lately the platypuses have been wandering farther and farther from the flats, and some folks are calling it the Invasion of the Platypus.

The platypus could be the blip. It works as a picture of the number 1, which is neither prime nor composite, and of all the real-world cases a tidy sorting system never planned for.

## The Market and the Makers

### 9. The Alphabet Bazaar

The Alphabet Bazaar is a covered market with twenty-six stalls running from A to Z, each one selling apps of a different sort. At A you can buy Apps, and at B everything comes in Binary pairs that are either on or off. The C stall sells Conduits (mostly pipes), and the D stall sells Digits, which turn out to be fingers, so the traveler can count their own, much to the annoyance of Digit the citizen. Game runs the Game stall, where three regulars, Jinx the Joker, the King, and Lou, play cards forever, and Indie fiddles at her tiny stall at I. At M you can trade an old Memory for a new one, at N everything is sold backward, and at S, Sal the crab is always having a sale on shiny thingamajigs. The V stall sells nothing but arrows called Vectors, and one of them looks just like the arrow on the box that brought the traveler here. Shoppers pay with dots from the mines.

### 10. Homebrew Hollow

Homebrew Hollow is a clubhouse inside the trunk of an enormous hollow tree, where tinkerers meet on Wednesday nights to build things from spare parts. Kettles bubble in every corner, and the walls are covered in buttons, switches, and blinking lights that a two-year-old would be very happy to press. Carved over the door is the club's only rule: "Build it, show it, share it." Everyone who makes something gives away the plans so the next person can make it better. Hob the Homebrewer keeps a garage burrow under the roots. Patch got his start here as a young spider, and Indie's apps, which Patch sells, were born here too. The hollow is a nod to the Homebrew Computer Club.

### 11. Xerox Park

Xerox Park is a garden where everything is a copy of a copy. The first tree near the gate is bright and green, but each tree after it is a slightly fainter copy, and by the far end of the park the trees are pale gray ghosts. The ducks quack in echoes, and the companion spends a long time chasing one before realizing it is a photocopy.

Somewhere in the middle stands the one original tree, and in a little workshop beside it, long ago, someone invented windows you could point at and a mouse to do the pointing. The very first window was Wendell, who still comes back to visit, and the mouse still lives there and points at things for visitors. The park's name is a pun on Xerox PARC.

## The Edges

### 12. The Sifting Sands

The Sifting Sands are a golden beach where a bearded old man named Eratosthenes shakes a giant sieve all day long. First he shakes loose every multiple of two, which falls through the holes, then every multiple of three, then five, and so on. Whatever is left glittering on the mesh is prime, and he sends it up the path to the Scissor Gate. Eratosthenes once measured the whole size of the Earth with a stick and a shadow, and he will tell you about it whether you ask or not. The traveler can sift sand with a bucket of their own here.

### 13. Remainder Row

Remainder Row is a crooked little lane just past the Scissor Gate, where the bits left over when numbers do not divide evenly come to live. The cottages are built from scraps and leftovers, so no two windows match. The residents are friendly and a bit scruffy, and they keep everything nobody else wants, including bread crusts, broken crayons, and single socks. Lou runs the Lost and Found at the end of the lane, where the shelves are full of books stamped "Property of the Lost Library, reward upon return." If you look closely, one of the socks hanging on a clothesline is the very sock Brock was glowering at on that long and lonesome hill.

### 14. The Zero Knowledge Grotto

The Zero Knowledge Grotto is a ring-shaped cave with two tunnels that meet at a magic door deep inside, and only a secret word opens it. A stern owl guards the entrance, but the owl does not want to hear the secret word, only to be sure that you know it. Zara the Zebra is the grotto's regular, and she has passed the owl's test more times than anyone can count. You walk in down either tunnel, the owl calls out "Come out on the left!" or "Come out on the right!", and if you know the word you can always do it. After twenty times in a row the owl is completely convinced, yet the owl still has no idea what the word is.

This is the classic cave story that cryptographers use to explain zero-knowledge proofs, and the traveler's secret word might well be their own first word in Primeland, like "Box!"

### 15. Big O Corral

Big O Corral is a ranch on the prairie with a giant O-shaped fence, run by Big O, the rolling wheel with a stopwatch face, who times how long every roundup takes as the herd grows. One cowpoke simply rings a bell and every sheep comes at once, no matter how many there are. Another counts the sheep one by one, so twice the sheep takes twice as long. A third insists on introducing every sheep to every other sheep, and as the herd gets bigger that cowpoke is still at it long after dark. The parents in the room will get the Big O notation joke.

### 16. The Electronic Frontier

The Electronic Frontier is the far edge of Primeland, where the paved grid stops and the land is still being drawn. The hills there flicker between sketch and color, and the maps have blank spaces labeled "not yet." A lone outpost called the Freedom Foundation keeps watch, and its rangers keep the trails open to every traveler and stand up for anyone who gets pushed around. Fern of the Frontier keeps her covered wagon parked beside it between expeditions past the edge. They are the stewards of the place, and they plant trees whose shade they will never sit in. The outpost is a nod to the EFF, and it is a good spot for the book's bigger question about what we build that lasts.

### 17. Terminus

Terminus lies on the far side of the Data Lake, and it is the central terminal where every Robo-Seal report eventually arrives. It is a grand old railway station with a vaulted glass roof, but its departures board is really a giant computer screen with a blinking green cursor, waiting for someone to type. Trains leave from Terminus for every sector of Primeland, including N1, N3, and N4. The tracks to Sector N2 shimmer and flicker like heat on a summer road, and no train has left for N2 in a long while. N2 lies in Nega's upside-down neighborhood, on the other side of Terminus.
