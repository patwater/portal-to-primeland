# Primeland Character Backgrounds

Nov 9, 2026 · Patrick Atwater

## How the roster works

The roster has 39 entries: two travelers, five keepers or keeper pairs, and 32 citizens, 31 of them drawn from the A-to-Z sketch page and one, Pip the Platypus, added later as the first new citizen (she takes badge 131). A short list of folk without badges follows the roster, for the people and creatures the locations mention who are not citizens, and they are not part of the count. Each citizen turns one tech or math idea into a creature a small child can point at, with a grown-up joke folded inside, in the same spirit as Snap who cuts corners and Snip who cuts slack.

Patch says that everything under the sun has been pre-assigned a unique prime, so every citizen wears a prime badge. The badge follows a simple rule: the letter's place in the alphabet picks the prime, so A wears 2, B wears 3, and Z wears 101. The second column on the sketch page (Qubit, Rate, Sale, True, Total) takes the next primes after 101, which makes 101 and 103 a pair of twin primes, and 107 and 109 another.

The traveler and companion have no badge at all, and that is the point of them. In the first draft, when the scissors ask for her count, Taylor says "Gray!", and a newcomer with no number is exactly who can help with a problem that is neither approved nor rejected.

For the microsite, each entry carries the same fields: badge, name, look (for drawing), background, catchphrase, and story hook. The citizens table can feed a character-card page directly.

Outside the worked example below, the hooks say "the traveler" and "the companion" rather than Taylor and Scout, because every family puts its own kid and pet in those roles. In the first-draft edition, the traveler is Taylor and the companion is Scout. Where a citizen lives, the place named is one of the places in `locations.md`. The world's voice and values come from `SOUL.md`, which also keeps the single list of open threads.

## The travelers

The portal opens for any kid who finds the box, so the traveler is a role rather than one fixed character. Each family can put its own child and companion in this slot, with their own shoes, their own first word, and their own answer to "What's your count?" Taylor and Scout are one possible pair, written up below as the worked example from the first draft.

### Taylor, one possible traveler

**Badge:** none. **Look:** a tiny tot in a dotted outfit and Crocs, always mid-run.

Taylor is the little wailer, the girl her dad always said he would put in a package and mail somewhere, and then went ahead and mailed. She runs up the hill with the verve and vigor of a tall ship sailor, and she has no time for why, where, or to what end. She sees a box where Brock saw packaging, and that difference is her whole superpower. In Primeland she is the only one who does not already have a number, so she is the only one who can say "Gray!" and mean it.

**Says:** "Box!"

**Story hook:** Primeland runs on yes or no, approved or rejected. Taylor is comfortable in the in-between, which is exactly where the trouble in sector N2 lives.

### Scout the Watchdog

**Badge:** none, though his watch keeps trying to assign him one. **Look:** Taylor's four-legged friend with a smartwatch tangled around his collar and one front paw, its little screen glowing on his chest.

Scout stumbled into Taylor, and the two of them tumbled into the box together. Somewhere in the portal he got tangled up in a smartwatch that had fallen out of the delivery cube, and he came out the other side as a watchdog. The watch counts his steps and his tail wags, buzzes when his heart races, and pops up little alerts nobody asked for ("You have been sniffing for 4 minutes. Time to stand!"). Scout finds this mortifying, because he is a nervous dog who wags in short spurts, and now everyone can see the numbers.

The grown-up joke is that a watchdog in a computer is a little timer that notices when something has frozen and gives it a nudge to restart. Scout is that kind of dog. He notices first, worries first, and barks when something has stopped moving.

**Says:** "Woof?" (the watch translates it as "Are we sure about this?")

**Story hook:** When the blip in N2 flickers and hangs, neither stamped nor rejected, it is Scout's watch that starts buzzing a steady countdown. The watchdog's job is to notice a stuck thing and nudge it, and that may be how the quandary gets unstuck.

## Keepers of Primeland

### The Adding Machine

**Badge:** 1, which is not a prime, so the Adding Machine lives just outside the Scissor Gate and never quite inside. **Look:** an old desk adding machine with a big side crank, round number keys, and a long paper tape curling out of its top.

The Adding Machine is the oldest thing in Primeland, older than the scissors and older than Patch's web. It can only add. It cannot subtract, divide, or forget, so its paper tape just keeps growing, and that tape is the whole history of Primeland, one sum at a time. The ticker tape wrapped around the bubble pile that says "welcome to Primeland" is a strip torn from its roll.

The grown-up joke is that the Adding Machine sees the world the opposite way from everyone else. The scissors cut numbers down into primes, which is how numbers come apart. The Adding Machine builds every number out of ones, which is how numbers go together. Both are true, and the two sides have not spoken in years.

**Says:** "Ka-chunk! And one more makes..."

**Story hook:** When the traveler asks "What is 4?", Patch is too busy to answer, but the Adding Machine cranks out 2 + 2 on its tape. It is the one keeper who will happily count a newcomer who has no prime.

### Alpha and Beta

**Badge:** none, since Greek letters come before numbers. **Look:** two young mountain goats with version stickers on their horns. Alpha's horns are lopsided, because she was the first try. Beta's horns are nearly right, and he will tell you so.

Alpha and Beta are always testing each other. Alpha builds something rough and fast and shoves it at Beta. Beta tries it, finds what breaks, and hands it back with notes. Then Beta builds something, and Alpha tests it twice as hard to get even. They butt heads all day, and it is how they both get better.

Their favorite game is the A or B test. They hold up two of anything, two hats, two paths, two snacks, and ask whoever walks by to pick. They run their tests in Protocol Plaza and keep a careful tally of every answer.

**Says:** Alpha: "Test you!" Beta: "Test you back!" Both: "A or B?"

**Story hook:** The traveler picks neither and asks for C. This breaks their tally, delights them both, and gives them a brand new test to run.

### Snip and Snap

**Badge:** they hand out badges rather than wearing them. **Look:** a single pair of scissors with a face on each blade.

Snip and Snap are cousins and the gatekeepers of Primeland. Snap cuts corners and Snip cuts slack, and the scissors promise you'll need both. They keep the Scissor Gate, just past the bubble-wrap landing. Their job is to cut every number down until it cannot be divided any further, because only then is it allowed into Primeland. They take the rules very seriously, or at least one of them does.

The grown-up joke is the question nobody asks: who cuts the cutters? A pair of scissors is two, and two is prime, so there is nothing to cut them down into. They are indivisible, and they are quietly proud of it. But each blade by itself is a one, the Adding Machine's badge, so the cousins are made of exactly what the Adding Machine counts. They have never said so out loud.

**Says:** "What's your count?"

**Story hook:** In the first draft, Taylor pokes them in the center spot, says "Gray!", and runs off. Every traveler answers "What's your count?" in their own way. They are still shouting about proper protocols when she disappears down the path. A traveler who asks the scissors "How do you get cut?" gets a long, fussy, flustered answer ending in "We are a pair. A pair is two. Two is prime. Nobody cuts us," and a very small silence where one blade looks at the other.

### Patch the Webmaster

**Badge:** none, because he says the webmaster is the web itself and not a node on it. **Look:** a cool and strange spider in a smart smock, with eight metallic legs and fine fiber strung across the whole data lake.

Patch is the webmaster, the Nodebug Humbug, and the Purveyor of Indie Apps. He keeps the data lake fully connected so information flows point to point, and he takes enormous pride in a sorting system where everything has its own prime. He patches whatever breaks, which is how he got his name, and he finds anything that is neither one thing nor the other deeply upsetting.

**Says:** "It's a very sophisticated system."

**Story hook:** Patch needs to go to Terminus to fix the blip in sector N2. He means to go alone, and the traveler decides otherwise.

### The Robo-Seals

**Badge:** each one is made of many tiny robots, so each seal is a product of many small parts. **Look:** dog-like swimmers in the data lake, built from little robots, with a stamp on each front flipper.

The Robo-Seals are the seal of approval. They swim the data lake inspecting everything, send what they see back to Terminus, the central terminal, and then stamp it. They stamp approval with their feet and stamp rejection with their tails. They have never once met something they could not stamp, until now.

**Says:** "Ork ork, approved!" or a very sad tail-slap.

**Story hook:** The seals swarm and yelp when they find the blip in N2, because it will not hold still long enough to be stamped either way.

## Folk without badges

These people and creatures turn up in the story and in `locations.md`, but none of them wears a prime badge. Patch says everything under the sun has been pre-assigned a unique prime, and this list shows that his system is less complete than he thinks, which is useful story fuel. They are not part of the 39-entry roster.
